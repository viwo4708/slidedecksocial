About

Slide Deck Social is a project built by Vikki Wong while studying Creative Technology and Design at the ATLAS Institute at CU Boulder.

In the modern day, technology has a way of isolating and separating people, and she hopes that she can use technology to instead bring people together and give them ways to connect in real life, beyond the screens.

The game was inspired by other digital social games such as Jackbox Games (particularly Talking Points), Photo Roulette, Gartic Phone, and Kahoot, as well as the good old fashioned Powerpoint Night. The goal was to create something easily accessible online, that was available for free for people to play.

Under the hood, this application runs on Node.JS, Express, and WebSocket.

Shout out to Jake Derouin for hosting this game on a server in his closet, and providing a lot of guidance and assistance with bringing this game to the public! This would not have been possible without him.

FAQ

Q: What happens to the topics and photos after a game ends?
A: All photos and player created topics are deleted from the server after the game ends, and they are not stored anywhere.

Q: Can players from other games see our photos and topics?
A: No, they can’t. All photos and topics are viewable only by players in the same game.

Q: Do we need to download anything from an app store?
A: Nope! Currently the game is fully accessible, functional, and free as a web app.

Q: How many people can play at once?
A: The game is designed for 3-8 players, although it will technically run with 2 people.

Q: What kind of photos should I upload?
A: That’s a question only you can answer….

Q: Does everyone need to be on the same WiFi network?
A: Nope! As long as everyone is on the internet or mobile data somehow, they can join.

Q: Can we play remotely over Zoom or Discord?
A: Yes!

Q: What happens if someone’s phone disconnects or I refresh the page?
A: You should be able to rejoin the game automatically, or by typing in the same room code and username that you used to join initially.

Q: How was AI used in the development of this application?
A: AI was used to research code architecture and structures, to learn about ways to solve problems or implement features, and to write code blocks and unit tests to implement and debug the game. AI was NOT used in the design of the game, the ideation process, the styling of the application, the writing of the in-game phrases, or as a substitute for user testing with real humans.
